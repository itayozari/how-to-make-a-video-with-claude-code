# The Jerv script, as recorded

About 60 words, about 25 seconds, one narrator. The opening line changed twice on the timeline before it became a question.

```
Why is getting paid the hardest part of the job?

Meet Jerv.

Jerv handles the awkward messages, so you can focus on the work.

You deliver. Jerv takes it from there.

It reminds your client when the invoice is due, and follows up until it's paid.

Jerv asks. You work.

So you can focus on what matters.

Try it now, it's free.
```

Earlier openings, in order: "Freelancer, spending your days chasing clients?" and "Spending your days chasing clients?". Both described what was already on screen. The question that replaced them names the pain.

## The shot plan

| Beat | On screen | How it was made |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The question | Tom at his desk on a grey afternoon, deleting a message and putting the phone down (1.6 s of a 6 s shot) | Seedance 2.5, from the character sheet and the grey room still |
| The pain on a screen | A phone's composer typing "Just checking in about the invoice..." and deleting it | Motion code |
| The name | "Meet" lands, the mark rolls out the name | Motion code |
| The promise | Two headlines, one word of each marked as it is spoken | Motion code |
| The mechanism | A deliver button swallows the cut and turns into the mark; the reminder, the client's answer, the receipt | Motion code |
| The turn | "Jerv asks. You work." over the cut to the after | Voice |
| The after | Tom at the same desk at golden hour, the camera pulling back out through the window (5 s of a 10 s shot), the closing line rising word by word | Seedance 2.5; the line is a motion overlay |
| The call to action | The mark turns in, the name slides out, "Try for free" rises and a cursor clicks it on the last word | Motion code |

Straight cuts everywhere; the pieces carry their own transitions. The picture is cut to the words: the name lands as it is said, the click comes on "deliver", the receipt on "paid", the cut to the after on the turn.
