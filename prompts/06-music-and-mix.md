# 06 · Music and the mix

Ask Claude to do the sound, and it knows what to do. Tonpit's skills carry the method: it searches the music library, turns on an EQ that clears room for the voice, cuts the song on a bar, ends it with a reverb tail, and places sound effects on the animation's moves. Each of those is also a switch in the music panel if you'd rather do it by hand.

When you know what you want, be specific:

```
End the song on the last bar of the verse, right where the picture ends, with a reverb tail. Keep the music under the voice, and put sound effects only on the big moves.
```

## Choosing the track

This is where your ear matters most. Music changes the whole feel of a video, and the right track takes many tries, or luck.

Fit the song by its ending. Find a natural end, usually the end of the song or of a verse, and line it up with the end of the picture. The start is more forgiving, so even an aggressive cut there works.

In the Jerv ad the track comes in under the "before" shot, which plays nearly dry on its own sound (rain, keys, a breath), and is full by the click on "Deliver". It ends about a second and a half before the picture, on a bar, with the tail under "Try it now, it's free."

## Sound effects

Claude's choices won't always sound right. Ask it to replace the ones you dislike, or pick from the effects library yourself. A useful rule from this cut: about one effect per 0.6 seconds of motion, never more than three on one moment.
