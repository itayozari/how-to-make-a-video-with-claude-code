# 05 · Making the animation look professional

The middle of the ad is animation written as code: "Meet Jerv", two headlines, a deliver button that turns into the logo, a short chat with the client. The first rounds worked and looked amateur. Notes help, and "it doesn't work for me yet" is a complete note. This prompt does the most:

```
Review this animation the way a world-class motion designer would. First simplify: cut anything that doesn't serve the message. Then refine the type, the spacing, the colour and every movement. Give every move intent and professional easing: anticipation before the big ones, overlap, and a tight stagger between elements. Nothing moves linearly, and no sequence opens on an empty frame. Then check it frame by frame and fix whatever still feels amateur.
```

Run it as many times as you need. A reference does even more: give Claude the pieces of an animation you admire and it will follow them.

## The direction that shaped the Jerv animation

The brief for the middle, before the first serious pass:

1. "Meet" enters big and settles back; the logo and the name join it. Look at the best motion-design and typography ads.
2. One headline that says what Jerv does, in one sentence.
3. An abstract product moment: a cursor presses "Deliver", the cut is sent to the client.
4. A short chat: the reminder about the invoice, the client's answer.
5. Abstract, minimal design that shows a feeling. Every letter, bubble and emoji gets attention. Dynamic camera moves, with transitions between sequences.

The notes over the next four rounds, as rules:

- **Nothing changes size without a reason.** The logo winds up, then rolls to the right with squash and stretch to reveal the name, instead of a mask wipe.
- **No sequence opens empty.** Something is always on screen across a transition.
- **Split long lines.** The headline became two, with an animated transition between them.
- **It's an ad, not UI.** The click on Deliver gets a designed moment: the card folds away, the button turns into the logo.
- **Kill the cheap effects.** A radial burst read as amateur. Organic shapes, a stagger and a better curve fixed it; flat shading made it belong.
- **Then cut the time.** The whole middle was shortened by about a third without feeling rushed: one bounce instead of two, the camera dives in earlier, the check mark and the arrow went.
